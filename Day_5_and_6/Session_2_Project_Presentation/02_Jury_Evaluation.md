# Project Presentation: Jury Evaluation

## Overview
Presentations will be evaluated by a panel of judges (the instructor and potentially guest industry experts). The evaluation process is designed to mimic performance reviews in a corporate setting, focusing on both technical execution and business value.

## Evaluation Criteria

The Jury will score each team based on a rubric divided into three main categories:

### 1. Technical Competence & Execution (40%)
- **Data Prep & Accuracy:** Was the data cleaned properly? Are the calculations and metrics accurate?
- **Dashboard Design:** Is the dashboard intuitive, visually appealing, and easy to navigate? Are the chosen charts appropriate for the data?
- **Advanced Features:** Did the team utilize interactive features (slicers, drill-downs, tooltips) effectively?
- **Code Quality (Backend):** (Evaluated from submitted documentation) Is the SQL/Python code well-structured and commented?

### 2. Business Impact & Insight Quality (40%)
- **Problem Resolution:** Did the analysis actually address the assigned problem statement?
- **Depth of Insight:** Did the team go beyond surface-level observations to find root causes?
- **Actionability:** Are the final recommendations practical, specific, and tied directly to the data? 
- **The "So What?" Factor:** Did the team successfully translate data points into business value?

### 3. Presentation & Communication Skills (20%)
- **Clarity & Delivery:** Was the presentation clear, confident, and engaging? Did all members participate equally?
- **Storytelling:** Was there a logical flow from the problem statement to the final recommendations?
- **Time Management:** Did the team stay within the allocated 15-minute time limit?
- **Handling Q&A:** How well did the team defend their decisions and answer questions on the spot?

## The Q&A Session
After the 15-minute presentation, the Jury will ask questions for 5 minutes. 
- Be prepared to explain *why* you chose a specific chart type or *how* you calculated a specific metric.
- If you don't know the answer, it is better to say, "That's a great question; we didn't look into that specific variable, but our hypothesis would be..." rather than making up an answer.
