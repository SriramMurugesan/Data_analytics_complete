# Project Presentation: Feedback Session

## Overview
Continuous improvement is a core principle of Data Analytics. The feedback session immediately follows the jury evaluations and is arguably the most valuable part of the capstone experience. It provides actionable advice for your future career.

## Structure of the Feedback Session

### 1. Immediate Jury Feedback
After all teams have presented, the jury will provide general feedback to the entire cohort.
- **Common Strengths:** Highlighting techniques or presentation styles that worked particularly well across multiple teams.
- **Areas for Improvement:** Identifying common pitfalls (e.g., "Many teams used pie charts when bar charts would have been clearer," or "Make sure to double-check your baseline metrics").

### 2. Individual Team Debriefs
Depending on time constraints, teams may receive a brief individual debrief or a written evaluation rubric returned to them.
- **Constructive Criticism:** Expect honest feedback on both your dashboard design and your presentation skills. Don't take it personally; the goal is to prepare you for industry standards.
- **Technical Review:** Instructors may point out more efficient ways to write the SQL queries or DAX measures you used in your project.

### 3. Peer Feedback
Learning from your peers is incredibly valuable. You will be asked to fill out a short, anonymous feedback form for the other presenting teams.
- What did you like most about their dashboard?
- What was the most compelling part of their presentation?
- What is one thing they could improve?

## Receiving Feedback Professionally
- **Listen Actively:** Take notes when the jury is speaking about your project.
- **Don't Get Defensive:** If a judge questions your data, explain your methodology calmly. If you made a mistake, own it ("You're right, we missed that null value in the dataset, which skews that metric. We will correct that in the final documentation.").
- **Apply It:** Use this feedback to update your project repository before putting it on your GitHub or portfolio. The "V2" of your capstone should reflect the jury's advice.
